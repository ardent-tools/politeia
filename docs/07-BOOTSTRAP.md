# Bootstrap and institutional reconnaissance

The first deployment phase establishes an institution-owner-approved discovery scope and performs read-only reconnaissance under a bounded, expiring grant inside a client-controlled environment. A recurring friction may guide discovery but is not a prerequisite for approving institutional knowledge. Only systems and ontology axes inside that grant may be inspected; expanding the scope requires a new explicit approval. The public product initializes a client-owned `InstitutionWorkspace`; private commissioner systems are neither the data plane nor a commissioning dependency.

## Sequence

1. Establish the client trust boundary, institution owner, temporary commissioner delegation, and private workspace.
2. Discover the connected systems and topology named by the approved scope.
3. Inventory the bounded repositories, services, data systems, identities, documentation, workflows, and policy surfaces named by the reconnaissance grant.
4. Extract observations with exact provenance.
5. Infer candidate authorities and institutional claims.
6. Detect contradictions, stale authorities, duplicated definitions, and missing ownership.
7. Record confidence and `missed_axes`.
8. Present a candidate institutional model.
9. Require institution-owner approval for constitutional commitments and unresolved authority choices.
10. Compile authorized context and capability discovery from the approved model; build and test the required adapter, pack, policy, and evidence obligations.
11. Enter shadow mode and calibrate detectors and bindings.
12. Derive and verify an immutable operational runtime generation; qualify its blocking controls under the [policy assurance contract](06-POLICY_COMPILER.md), then activate it under an institution-owner decision bound to the complete assurance evidence.
13. Hand off the preserved workspace and generation, then revoke or expire commissioner authority without interrupting ordinary operation.
14. Preserve operational feedback as observations and proposed corrections; require authorized approval before those proposals change institutional truth or the active generation.

## Epistemic states

`Observation → InferredClaim → ContestedClaim | ApprovedFact`

No inference is silently promoted to approved truth.

Approval resolves the exact candidate, source observations, evidence, institution, workspace, and approving authority. A wire label, registered signing key, claimed owner identifier, or caller-supplied corroboration count is insufficient. Verification authenticates canonical, domain-separated bytes against installed trust anchors and then checks the signer's authority for the requested admission. A signature is evidence of identity, not a grant of authority.

Recommissioning deliberately returns to a broader engineering profile under a new explicit delegation. It does not reconstruct deleted source or restore an ambient commissioner.
